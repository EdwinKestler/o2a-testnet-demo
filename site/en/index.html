O2A — demonstration on Bitcoin signet

An artist creates an identity that depends on no platform, uses it to sign a live-event attestation, and two verifiers that do not trust each other reach the same result using only Bitcoin.

It is not in production. This demonstration is not a release, and mainnet is out of scope.
It creates no persistent identity. Today's identities are tests and will be discarded.
It does not replace ticketing. It makes no claim to replace today's ticket-sales systems.

Every identity created by this demonstration is disposable. Before creation, every participant is given this warning and must explicitly acknowledge it.

01 / The sequence

What you will see

Three acts make the identity, a signed statement, and independent verification visible. The Bitcoin, RGB, and O2A results remain separate during evaluation.

Act 1 · identity

What you see

An artist identity already anchored on signet, its identifier, and the containing block. If decision D5 permits it, a controller rotation appears in the explorer.

What happens underneath

The demo presents an RGB genesis and, optionally, a rotation transition together with its signed O2A object.

Act 2 · signing

What you see

The artist signs the object selected in D2. A transferable proof package is produced and its anchor is broadcast to signet.

What happens underneath

The demo encodes and signs an object, links it to this run's RGB state, and waits for Bitcoin confirmation.

Act 3 · agreement

What you see

Two verifiers receive the same package and display separate Bitcoin, RGB, and O2A authorization results side by side.

What happens underneath

Each process evaluates the supplied evidence with its own Bitcoin connection; the evaluation core does not query an O2A service.

02 / Shared language

Glossary

Technical termHow we say it in the demo
EntityIDThe artist's identity: a unique identifier controlled only by them.
ControllerThe key the artist signs with today; it can rotate without losing the identity.
AttestationA statement signed by the artist: “I confirm X.”
Bitcoin seal / anchorThe moment the statement is fixed in Bitcoin and can no longer be altered.
Proof packageThe file anyone can verify without connecting to us.
VerifierAn independent program that reports “valid” or “invalid” without asking us.
SignetBitcoin's test network: blocks every 10 minutes and valueless test bitcoin.

03 / The case

Why it matters

The exact commercial framing remains pending; these are the problems the script proposes making visible.

Fake promoters

A verifiable statement distinguishes something the artist signed from something they did not sign.

Impersonation and direct sales

The demo shows participant-controlled keys and a rotation without replacing the identifier being presented.

Traceability

Artists, venues, and promoters can present verifiable cross-signatures; the specific actors remain pending.

Portability

The package moves between verifiers; checking it does not depend on a central O2A interface.

05 / Try it

Verify it yourself

Obtain the package from pending path: EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_DIR. The event has not yet produced a public signet package.

The exact interface advertised by the CLI is:

o2a-demo verify-package VALIDATOR_DIR ELECTRUM_URL EVIDENCE_PACKAGE_DIR

The verifier needs a fresh directory, an Electrum endpoint, and the package directory. This line comes directly from crates/o2a-demo-cli/src/main.rs:361.

06 / Conversation

Questions

Is this crypto or tokens?

There is no O2A token. The demonstration uses Bitcoin to order and anchor evidence.

What does each signature cost?

Signing happens locally. Anchoring uses a signet transaction, whose test bitcoin has no value. Mainnet is out of scope.

What if the artist loses their keys?

Recovery is not demonstrated today. The specification—not this page—is the authority for that design.

Why not use our database?

Because the scene is meant to show two processes verifying the same package without querying an O2A service.

When will this be in production?

No date is promised. This work uses a release candidate of RGB and is disposable evidence.
